KRAFTON India has built a competition where the fans do the scoring. The BGMI Survivor League puts 50 creators on one leaderboard for 30 days, and the standings are driven by how much their communities play.

The event runs from October 18 to November 17 under the banner The Final Mahayudh: Survivor League. The prize pool is ₹70 lakh, with ₹12 lakh for first place and a guaranteed minimum of ₹50,000 for every creator. Together, the 50 creators reach 69.7 million subscribers.

The mechanics are simple. Fans join a creator's team inside BGMI, then earn points for that creator through daily play and in-game missions. The leaderboard updates weekly. On the weekend of October 24 and 25, fans could switch teams and carry their earned points with them, a window KRAFTON calls The Switch. The Grand Finale decides the final order after scores are verified.

The creators are split into four subscriber-based tiers, from five Elite creators with more than four million subscribers each to 22 rising creators with between 100,000 and 500,000. Everyone competes on the same leaderboard, so a smaller creator with an active community can outrank a larger channel.

There is no entry fee and no staking. KRAFTON describes the league as a skill-based contest played inside BGMI; fans receive in-game rewards for taking part, while the prize money goes to the creators.

It is not a traditional esports tournament. There are no brackets, no LAN, and no team houses. It is a creator campaign built around daily engagement, and the leaderboard is designed to reward the creators who can keep their audiences playing. The final results will be announced after the event closes and KRAFTON verifies the scores.
