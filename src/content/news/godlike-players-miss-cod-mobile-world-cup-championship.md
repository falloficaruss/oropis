GodLike Esports is facing another World Championship visa crisis. Nishad “Flirt” Gavali said on November 3 that Mexico had refused his visa application, along with the applications of teammates Manas “VipeR” Dhruv and Aniket “SkullG” Majumdar.

The refusals leave three of GodLike’s players unable to make the planned trip to Mexico City for the Call of Duty: Mobile World Championship Finals, which begin on December 4. The event has a $500,000 prize pool and brings together 16 teams from eight regions. GodLike qualified as India’s second seed; Galorys took the region’s other place.

Flirt described representing India at the international event as a personal ambition and said a month of preparation now appeared to have been lost. VipeR responded with a blunt joke: perhaps the World Championship should be held in India instead.

For GodLike, the timing creates an immediate roster problem. CODM’s World Championship uses five-player lineups, with one optional substitute. With three players reporting visa refusals, the organisation needs enough eligible players to travel and field a legal team, while the Finals are only a month away. GodLike has not publicly announced a replacement plan.

## A repeat problem for GodLike

This is not the first time travel paperwork has disrupted GodLike’s international campaign. The team withdrew from the 2024 Snapdragon Pro Series Masters in São Paulo after Brazilian visas were not processed in time. In 2025, GodLike and S8UL both ran into visa problems before the World Championship in Katowice, Poland; the organisations ultimately combined available players under the Newgen Ninjas name.

GodLike returned for 2026 and earned its Worlds place through India’s circuit. The team won the India Points Major in July, topped the India Circuit Points standings and secured the second regional slot despite losing the Regional Finals to Galorys. It also carries considerable World Championship pedigree: GodLike finished runner-up to Wolves at the 2023 Finals.

The latest setback is especially difficult because it is happening after qualification, rather than during it. GodLike has already completed the competitive work required to reach the Finals. It now has to solve a travel and roster question that is separate from its performance in the game.

## Why the Mexico route matters

Mexico’s rules offer a narrow shortcut for some Indian travellers. The Mexican Embassy in India says that holders of valid, multiple-entry visas for the United States, Canada, Japan, the United Kingdom or the Schengen Area may enter Mexico for short business, tourism or transit visits without first obtaining a Mexican visa. Travellers who do not have one of those documents must apply directly for a Mexican visa.

The exemption does not resolve every issue. The embassy also makes clear that a visa or exemption document does not guarantee admission at the border; Mexican immigration authorities make the final decision on entry. For a team travelling for a publisher-run tournament, that means invitations, accommodation, return travel and team documentation all still need to be in order.

Those rules help explain why late refusals are so disruptive. A player who receives a refusal close to an international event does not simply lose practice time. The organisation may need to identify a replacement who is eligible under the tournament rules and able to travel on the same schedule, then give that player enough time to integrate with the team.

## The wider question for CODM esports

The case also exposes a planning problem that reaches beyond one team. International esports events rely on players from countries with very different visa requirements and processing times. A regional final held only weeks before a global LAN can leave teams with little room to respond when an application is delayed or refused.

Publishers and tournament organisers cannot control consular decisions, but they can reduce the risk. Earlier travel support for likely qualifiers, clearer documentation packages and contingency rules for emergency replacements would all give teams more time to react. The 2025 Poland problems and the current Mexico refusals show why that work has to happen before the final qualifier, not after it.

For now, the immediate issue is simpler: whether GodLike can assemble a travel-eligible lineup for Mexico City. The team has a World Championship slot, but its route to using it is suddenly much narrower.
